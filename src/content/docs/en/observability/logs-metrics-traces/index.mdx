You cannot fix what you cannot see. This chapter explores the three foundational pillars of observability and, more importantly, how correlating these distinct signals allows engineers to quickly move from a high-level system alert down to the exact line of failing code.

### Topics Covered

* **[8.1.1. Structured Logging: JSON Logs and Correlation IDs](/distributed-systems/en/observability/logs-metrics-traces/structured-logging/)**: Explains why plain-text logs are obsolete in distributed systems and the necessity of machine-readable, context-rich logging.
* **[8.1.2. Log Aggregation: ELK Stack (Elasticsearch, Logstash, Kibana)](/distributed-systems/en/observability/logs-metrics-traces/log-aggregation-elk/)**: Covers the architecture required to centralize, index, and search millions of log lines generated across hundreds of nodes.
* **[8.1.3. Metrics: Counter, Gauge, Histogram, Summary](/distributed-systems/en/observability/logs-metrics-traces/metrics/)**: Details the numeric data structures used to measure system behavior and resource consumption over time efficiently.
* **[8.1.4. The RED Method (Rate, Errors, Duration) vs. the USE Method](/distributed-systems/en/observability/logs-metrics-traces/red-vs-use-method/)**: Contrasts the two primary frameworks for monitoring application performance versus underlying hardware utilization.
* **[8.1.5. Distributed Tracing: Span, Trace, and Baggage Propagation](/distributed-systems/en/observability/logs-metrics-traces/distributed-tracing/)**: Explores how to track a single user request as it hops across network boundaries, measuring latency at every stop.
* **[8.1.6. Correlation: Joining Three Signals from a Single Failure Event](/distributed-systems/en/observability/logs-metrics-traces/signal-correlation/)**: Discusses the ultimate goal of observability: seamlessly pivoting between a metric spike, the corresponding trace, and the specific error logs.
