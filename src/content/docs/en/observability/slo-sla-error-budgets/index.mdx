100% reliability is an impossible and mathematically expensive goal. This chapter bridges the gap between engineering and business by exploring Site Reliability Engineering (SRE) practices, defining what level of failure is acceptable, and using data to settle debates about whether to ship features or fix tech debt.

### Topics Covered

* **[8.3.1. SLI (Service Level Indicator): What Should Be Measured?](/distributed-systems/en/observability/slo-sla-error-budgets/sli/)**: Defines the specific, objective metrics (like request latency or error rate) that truly represent user happiness.
* **[8.3.2. SLO (Service Level Objective): The Art of Setting Targets](/distributed-systems/en/observability/slo-sla-error-budgets/slo/)**: Covers how to define realistic, internal success thresholds for your indicators (e.g., "99.9% of requests will succeed in under 200ms").
* **[8.3.3. SLA (Service Level Agreement): Legal Obligations](/distributed-systems/en/observability/slo-sla-error-budgets/sla/)**: Explains the difference between an engineering target and a business contract that dictates financial penalties for downtime.
* **[8.3.4. Error Budget: Balancing Innovation and Reliability](/distributed-systems/en/observability/slo-sla-error-budgets/error-budget/)**: Details the cultural tool that uses the gap between 100% and your SLO to permit safe experimentation and feature releases.
* **[8.3.5. Toil: Measuring and Reducing Operational Burden](/distributed-systems/en/observability/slo-sla-error-budgets/toil/)**: Addresses the manual, repetitive work tied to running a production service and strategies for automating it away.
* **[8.3.6. Practical Lessons from the Google SRE Book](/distributed-systems/en/observability/slo-sla-error-budgets/google-sre/)**: Summarizes the foundational philosophies of SRE, including post-mortems, blameless culture, and incident response.
