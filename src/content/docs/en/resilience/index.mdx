In distributed architectures, failures are not exceptions; they are expectations. Hardware will crash, networks will partition, and unexpected traffic spikes will occur. This part covers the engineering practices and defensive patterns required to build resilient systems that anticipate these failures, ensuring that localized issues do not cascade into global outages.

### Chapters

* **[7.1. Flow Control and Overload Management](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/flow-control/)**: Explores how systems handle traffic beyond their capacity, covering backpressure, load shedding, and dynamic rate limiting.
* **[7.2. Protection Patterns](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/protection-patterns/)**: Details the defensive coding architectures, such as circuit breakers and bulkheads, that isolate faults and prevent resource exhaustion.
* **[7.3. Failure Detection and Membership Protocols](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/failure-detection/)**: Examines how clusters efficiently track healthy nodes and probabilistically detect silent failures using epidemic algorithms.
* **[7.4. Chaos Engineering](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/chaos-engineering/)**: Introduces the discipline of proactively injecting faults into production environments to uncover hidden vulnerabilities before they cause an incident.
