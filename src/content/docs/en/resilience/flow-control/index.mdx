When user demand or upstream requests exceed the physical limits of your infrastructure, the system must gracefully degrade rather than catastrophically crash. This chapter, contained in **index_50.mdx**, explores how to push back against overwhelming load, protect critical resources, and systematically drop traffic when necessary.

### Topics Covered

* **[7.1.1. Backpressure: Pushing Load Back to the Source](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/flow-control/backpressure/)**: Explains the mechanism of signaling upstream components to slow down rather than blindly buffering requests until memory runs out.
* **[7.1.2. Load Shedding: Which Requests to Drop and How](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/flow-control/load-shedding/)**: Details the deliberate strategy of rejecting incoming traffic at the edge to preserve the stability of the core system.
* **[7.1.3. Priority Queues: Protecting Critical Requests](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/flow-control/priority-queues/)**: Covers how to differentiate traffic types, ensuring that essential transactions survive even during severe overload conditions.
* **[7.1.4. Rate Limiting: Token Bucket, Leaky Bucket, Fixed and Sliding Window](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/flow-control/rate-limiting/)**: Analyzes the mathematical models and algorithms used to enforce strict traffic quotas.
* **[7.1.5. Adaptive Rate Limiting: Dynamic Limits](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/flow-control/adaptive-rate-limiting/)**: Explores advanced techniques where systems automatically adjust their own throttling thresholds based on real-time hardware metrics.
