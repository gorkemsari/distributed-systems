Defensive architecture requires proactive strategies that stop localized failures from consuming shared resources and taking down the entire system. This chapter details the implementation of protective design patterns that isolate faults, manage network delays, and safely retry operations.

### Topics Covered

* **[7.2.1. Circuit Breaker: The Closed to Open to Half-Open Cycle](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/protection-patterns/circuit-breaker/)**: Explains how to stop sending requests to an already failing downstream service, giving it time to recover.
* **[7.2.2. Bulkhead Pattern: Thread Pool and Semaphore Isolation](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/protection-patterns/bulkhead-pattern/)**: Details isolating components physically or logically so that exhaustion in one area (like an external API) does not drain the entire application's threads.
* **[7.2.3. Retry with Exponential Backoff and Jitter](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/protection-patterns/retry-exponential-backoff/)**: Covers the safe way to retry transient network failures without accidentally launching a self-inflicted Denial of Service (DoS) attack.
* **[7.2.4. Timeouts: Why They Are So Hard to Configure](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/protection-patterns/timeouts/)**: Analyzes the complexities of determining how long to wait for a response and the cascading dangers of infinite blocking.
* **[7.2.5. Fallback Strategies: Degraded Service, Cached Response](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/protection-patterns/fallback-strategies/)**: Explores how to keep the user experience intact by serving alternative or stale data when the primary operational path fails.
* **[7.2.6. From Hystrix to Resilience4j: Library Comparison](/distributed-systems/en/resilience/protection-patterns/hystrix-resilience4j/)**: Contrasts the evolution of resilience libraries, moving from heavy, thread-bound frameworks to modern, functional approaches.
