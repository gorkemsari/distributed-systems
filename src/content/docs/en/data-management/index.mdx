State is the hardest part of any distributed system. This part explores how we store, distribute, and maintain the integrity of data across multiple machines. We will cover the physical mechanics of splitting databases, replicating data for fault tolerance, and the theoretical models that dictate consistency and performance trade-offs.

### Chapters

* **[3.1. Data Partitioning / Sharding](/distributed-systems/en/data-management/data-partitioning/)**: Explores how to split massive datasets across multiple nodes, handle hot spots, and manage dynamic rebalancing.
* **[3.2. Replication Strategies](/distributed-systems/en/data-management/replication/)**: Covers leader-based and leaderless replication, quorum mechanics, and how to survive hardware failures without losing data.
* **[3.3. Consistency Models](/distributed-systems/en/data-management/consistency-models/)**: Deep dives into the trade-offs between strong linearizability and eventual consistency, exploring the CAP and PACELC theorems.
* **[3.4. Storage Engines and Data Structures](/distributed-systems/en/data-management/storage-engines/)**: Examines how databases actually write to disk, comparing LSM-Trees, B-Trees, and the importance of Write-Ahead Logs (WAL).
* **[3.5. Distributed Caching](/distributed-systems/en/data-management/distributed-caching/)**: Details architectural strategies for reducing latency and database load, handling cache invalidations, and preventing cache stampedes.
