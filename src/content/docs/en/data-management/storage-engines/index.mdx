Databases are not magical black boxes; at their core, they are highly optimized software designed to manage bytes on a disk. This chapter goes under the hood to explore the low-level data structures that dictate a system's performance, durability, and whether it is optimized for heavy write throughput, complex analytical queries, or rapid point lookups.

### Topics Covered

* **[3.4.1. LSM-Tree vs. B-Tree: Write and Read Performance Trade-offs](/distributed-systems/en/data-management/storage-engines/lsm-tree-vs-b-tree/)**: Compares the two fundamental storage architectures, analyzing why B-Trees dominate traditional relational databases while LSM-Trees power modern, write-heavy NoSQL systems.
* **[3.4.2. SSTables, Memtable, and Compaction Strategies](/distributed-systems/en/data-management/storage-engines/sstables-memtable-compaction/)**: Details the mechanics of log-structured storage, from in-memory buffers to immutable on-disk segments and background garbage collection.
* **[3.4.3. Bloom Filters: Reducing Disk Reads](/distributed-systems/en/data-management/storage-engines/bloom-filters/)**: Explains the space-efficient probabilistic data structure used to save expensive disk I/O operations by quickly answering whether a key *might* exist or *definitely* does not.
* **[3.4.4. Write-Ahead Log (WAL): The Foundation of Durability](/distributed-systems/en/data-management/storage-engines/write-ahead-log/)**: Explores the critical append-only log that protects data against sudden crashes and power failures before it is permanently flushed to main storage tables.
* **[3.4.5. Columnar Storage: Advantages in Analytical Queries](/distributed-systems/en/data-management/storage-engines/columnar-storage/)**: Analyzes the shift from row-oriented to column-oriented layouts, enabling massive compression and high-speed aggregation for OLAP and data warehouse workloads.
