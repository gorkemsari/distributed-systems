Testing a distributed system is fundamentally harder than testing a monolith, because the bugs live in the interactions -- the timeout that fires under load, the message that arrives twice, the replica that lags -- and none of them appear when services are tested in isolation. This chapter covers integration and contract testing at service boundaries, end-to-end testing and its flakiness, testing asynchronous and eventually-consistent flows, staging fidelity, load testing, and the correctness testing (Jepsen, deterministic simulation) that hunts for the consistency violations unit tests can never find.

* **[12.4.2. End-to-End Testing: Balancing Coverage and Flakiness](/distributed-systems/en/deployment/distributed-testing/end-to-end-testing/)**: Explains end-to-end testing and the balance between coverage and the flakiness that erodes trust.
* **[12.4.3. Testing Event-Driven Systems: Async Flows and Eventual Consistency](/distributed-systems/en/deployment/distributed-testing/testing-event-driven/)**: Covers testing asynchronous flows and eventual consistency where results arrive after the call returns.
* **[12.4.4. Staging Fidelity: How Realistic Is Your Test Environment?](/distributed-systems/en/deployment/distributed-testing/staging-fidelity/)**: Explains staging fidelity: how realistically a test environment reproduces production behavior.
