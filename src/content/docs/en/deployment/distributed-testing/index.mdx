Testing a monolith is straightforward; testing a business transaction that hops across five independent services, two message queues, and three databases is exceptionally difficult. This chapter explores the methodologies required to ensure distributed applications function correctly both in isolation and as a holistic system.

### Topics Covered

* **[12.4.1. Integration Testing: Service Boundaries and Contract Verification](/distributed-systems/en/deployment/distributed-testing/integration-testing/)**: Focuses on validating that APIs communicate correctly, utilizing consumer-driven contracts to catch breaking changes early.
* **[12.4.2. End-to-End Testing: Balancing Coverage and Flakiness](/distributed-systems/en/deployment/distributed-testing/end-to-end-testing/)**: Analyzes the high cost and fragility of full-system tests, and strategies to keep them fast, deterministic, and reliable.
* **[12.4.3. Testing Event-Driven Systems: Async Flows and Eventual Consistency](/distributed-systems/en/deployment/distributed-testing/testing-event-driven/)**: Addresses the unique challenge of asserting test outcomes when processes happen in the background and data updates asynchronously.
* **[12.4.4. Staging Fidelity: How Realistic Is Your Test Environment?](/distributed-systems/en/deployment/distributed-testing/staging-fidelity/)**: Discusses the trade-offs of maintaining a production-like staging environment versus utilizing localized tools like Testcontainers.
* **[12.4.5. Test Data Management: Synthetic Data, Anonymization, and Seeding](/distributed-systems/en/deployment/distributed-testing/test-data-management/)**: Explores how to generate, manage, and sanitize the massive datasets required to run meaningful and secure tests.
