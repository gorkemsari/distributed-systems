The companies that pioneered microservices did not adopt them for elegance; they adopted them because an organization of hundreds of teams could not ship on a single codebase. This chapter reads how Netflix, Uber, and Airbnb actually made that transition -- Netflix inventing chaos engineering to survive its own scale, Uber imposing domain-oriented structure on microservice sprawl, Airbnb migrating off a monolith incrementally -- and the cultural machinery (blameless postmortems, technical-debt management) that makes operating at that scale sustainable.

* **[13.3.2. Uber's Domain-Oriented Microservice Architecture (DOMA)](/distributed-systems/en/case-studies/microservices-at-scale/uber-doma/)**: Covers Uber's Domain-Oriented Microservice Architecture that imposed structure on microservice sprawl.
* **[13.3.3. Airbnb's Journey from Monolith to Microservices](/distributed-systems/en/case-studies/microservices-at-scale/airbnb-migration/)**: Traces Airbnb's incremental journey from a Rails monolith to a service-oriented architecture.
