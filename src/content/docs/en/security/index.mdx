In a distributed system the network perimeter is not a boundary you can trust -- traffic that looks internal may originate from a compromised service, so security must be enforced at every hop rather than at the edge. This part builds that model from identity outward: how services and users authenticate and authorize (OAuth, JWT, RBAC), the zero-trust principle of verifying every request regardless of origin, and the secrets-management and encryption discipline that protects data whether it is moving or at rest.

### Topics Covered

* **[11.1. Authentication and Authorization](/distributed-systems/en/security/authentication-authorization/)**: Identity for services and users: OAuth 2.0 and OIDC, JWT, service accounts, and the RBAC and ABAC authorization models.
* **[11.2. Zero Trust Architecture](/distributed-systems/en/security/zero-trust/)**: Never trust, always verify: micro-segmentation, identity-aware proxies, BeyondCorp, continuous verification, and policy as code.
* **[11.3. Secrets Management and Encryption](/distributed-systems/en/security/secrets-management/)**: Protecting credentials and data: Vault dynamic secrets, envelope encryption, encryption in transit and at rest, and supply-chain security.
