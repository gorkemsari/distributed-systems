Hardcoding passwords or leaving sensitive API keys in environment variables is a recipe for disaster. This chapter explores the infrastructure required to safely manage, dynamically generate, and rapidly rotate cryptographic keys and credentials at scale.

### Topics Covered

* **[11.3.1. HashiCorp Vault: Dynamic Secrets, Leases, and Rotation](/distributed-systems/en/security/secrets-management/hashicorp-vault/)**: Explores the industry-standard tool for centralized secrets management and its ability to generate ephemeral credentials on demand.
* **[11.3.2. Kubernetes Secrets: Why They Are Not Enough](/distributed-systems/en/security/secrets-management/kubernetes-secrets/)**: Highlights the security vulnerabilities of native Kubernetes secrets (base64 encoding) and how to properly integrate external secret stores.
* **[11.3.3. Envelope Encryption: The Key Encryption Key (KEK) Model](/distributed-systems/en/security/secrets-management/envelope-encryption/)**: Details the highly scalable cryptographic practice of encrypting data keys with a master key stored in a hardware security module (KMS/HSM).
* **[11.3.4. Encryption in Transit vs. At Rest: Both Are Mandatory](/distributed-systems/en/security/secrets-management/encryption-in-transit-at-rest/)**: Covers the operational mechanics of protecting data while it moves across the network and while it sits on physical storage.
* **[11.3.5. Supply Chain Security: The SLSA Framework and Sigstore](/distributed-systems/en/security/secrets-management/supply-chain-security/)**: Examines how to defend against compromised third-party dependencies and how to cryptographically sign container images to guarantee their origin.
